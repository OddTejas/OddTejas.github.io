How mathematicans cheat:

Transforming a function.

Problems involving the translation of a quadratic function usually begin the same way: find the vertex, convert the equation into vertex form, and only then move it horizontally or vertically.

Consider the following:

Translate the graph of function $y=2x^2+3x+1$ by two units in $x$-direction and by $-3$ units in y direction.

Solving this requires finding its vertex, either by calculus or algebraicly by completing the square. Then transforming the vertex by given values. Either way we need to write it in vertex form to do such operations.

This vertex form resembles the following $$a(x-h)^2+k$$ where $(h,k)$ reperesnt the coordinates of the vertex.

Is there any better way?

Converting like this is a hassle and we got better things to do. When I was sloving this I did get the answer but I was not satisfied as the process felt clumsy. When I was learning maths in intermediate, I was taught transformation of axes: Changing the origin of a graph.
I didnt think of it much, but I remembered seeing a method of subsitiuting values in the equation to transform its vertex. This looked like transformation of axes, but why would something like this work? We are moving the graph and not the curve and yet it work??!!

Now think, If you were looking at an object from front on, and suppose you needed to look it's other sides, you can rotate the object or change your position such that other side is visable, Either way the shape of object doesnt change only our prespecitve. Thus, what I thought was a clever 'trick' was actually really a clever implementation of transformation of axes, One can simply transform the prespective instead of doing the whole algebric gynmastics.

Proofs

The formula for transformation of axes is the followng $$x = X-h$$ $$y = Y-k$$ where x and y are coordinated in original axes and X, Y are new coordinates. if the original curve is $y=f(x)$ then the transformed equation simply is; $$y-k = f(x-h)$$ $$y=f(x-h)+k$$

Solving the original question

Given; $h=2$ and $y=-3$
$f(x)=2x^2+3x+1$
substituting h and k as $h=2$ $k=-3$
$$2(x-2)^2 +3(x-2)+1-3 $$ by simplification we arrive at: $$\boxed{2x^2 -5x}$$ Thus mathematican cheat by changing prespectives, and finding ways to simplify and solve easier.
